## Features ordered 2026-11-15 (architecture, not code)

Same form as the legal requirements: each line is one testable sentence with its id, stage, layer (**L1** Luminarkon, the
node layer and ledger; **C** the council; **R** the relay; **S** the site) and the decision or rule it rests on. "Built"
names the fork commit; a sentence whose test does not exist yet is "to be written" and no stage is passed on it.

### A1. The minor council's ticket pool and the 2/3 quorum (Stage 8, layer 2; rules: consensus and punishment, reports: the council judges only reported content)

Source: `.ai-docs/analysis/2026-11-14-spec-council-ticket-pool.md` (the maintainers' tree, sections 4 to 10) and the council layer 2 fork
(network `src/impl/council.ts`, `src/interfaces/council.ts`; the council layer 2 plan). The pool is a pure fold over signed records, so the same functions move into
the node's reducers unchanged.

The mechanism:

1. **A report becomes a ticket with the content and, of the author, only the fields named here.** A report is the new kind 8918 (`ang.report/1`,
   signed by the reporter's device key; spec section 4); the ticket carries the normalised text, a category from a closed
   set (offence, threat, racism, homophobia in the first release) and, of the author, only the login, display name, bio and
   photo (the reported content plus the author's login, display name, bio and photo, and only these; the original login is included), never the author's key as a field (A1.1) or any other field; for an author whose profile is private (A6.8, A2.15) it carries
   none of them (A6.11). Four more report categories,
   `child-abuse`, `intimate-image`, `grooming` and `abduction` (ECA Digital, Lei 15.211/2025, art. 27: exploitation, sexual
   abuse, abduction, grooming), never become a ticket: the report goes by id to the operator's procedure of Q50 (Q21, Q44)
   before any ticket exists, and Q55 fires on each of them as Q55 scopes it (`intimate-image` only when it shows a child or
   adolescent).
   This rule governs the author fields (closing the conflict of OI-20); the lawyer's review of those
   fields against LGPD minimisation is a pending item, never a second rule.
2. **Three record shapes, one kind.** Every council record is kind **8917** (the existing node kind), tag `v` = `ang.node/1`,
   tag `t` naming the record: `ticket-open` (tags `d` ticket id = sha256 of the canonical content, `x` sha256 of the normalised
   text, `cat`, `mf` the model-manifest digest, `k` target answers, `dl` deadline, `mm` margin minimum; content the text and, of the author, the login, display name, bio and photo of step 1 (none of them for a private profile, A6.11), except the guardian's authorization ticket of A6.11, whose content is the fields' digests only),
   `ticket-answer` (tags `d`, `mf`, `x`; content `label` in `illegal | legal | unclear`, `margin`, `out_sha256` of the
   model output and the `salt` of its commit, A1.11; on an A6.11 ticket also `pin`, the seed version whose lexicon its node ran, and `ocr` (true when that lexicon flags the photo's OCR text, A1.14)) and `ticket-conclude` (tag `d`; content `outcome`, `n` counted, `q` 1 when one miner ruled provisionally, and the signed `ticket-answer` records it counted, embedded whole, so the strike view of A1.8 and the slash proof of A1.9 read every counted label from it).
   The next slice adds `ticket-reverse` (an appeal or operator reversal, spec section 8) and `economic-params` (A3); the
   design adds `ticket-commit` (A1.11), `node-presence` (A1.10) and `ticket-delivery` (A6.11), so the three built shapes are not the closed set.
   See A1.10: `k`, `dl` and `mm` are derived from the network, never set by the ticket's author.
3. **States** are derived at read time from the events and never stored: `open` (no counted answer), `mining` (at least one),
   `concluded` (a conclusion equal to the tally was accepted), and `void`, for an A6.11 ticket only (one that cannot conclude, A6.11).
4. **Who mines.** A node holding a bond. Only bonded keys' answers count and an account behind two node keys counts once;
   an unbonded answer is refused `miner-not-bonded`. A miner runs the manifest's open model with fixed, signed weights.
   See A2.8: the inviter answers in network for the invitees' incorrect answers.
5. **The 2/3 quorum.** Of the first `k` counted answers (in the node-layer seal order of A1.12, never by `created_at`; the code built today orders by `created_at` then id and is replaced by A1.12), the outcome is the label with at
   least `ceil(2n/3)` answers; an answer whose margin is under `mm` counts as `unclear`; no label reaching the quorum is
   `unclear`. With one miner (`q=1`) that miner rules provisionally and nobody is punished, since there is no dissent.
   See A1.11 (commit, then reveal after `dl`) and A1.12 (order by the seal order, one definition of `n`).
6. **Refusals carry a code:** `bad-record`, `bad-signature`, `not-a-node-record`, `bad-category`, `bad-ticket`,
   `duplicate-ticket` (dedupe by text digest, except after an `unclear` conclusion, A1.14), `report-cap` (A1.16),
   `profile-private` (A6.11), `unknown-ticket`, `ticket-concluded`, `miner-not-seeded` (A1.13, checked first while fewer than 3
   PCs are online, and on an A6.11 ticket at any count), `miner-not-bonded` (checked next, in every state), `answer-mf-mismatch`,
   `answer-x-mismatch`, `bad-answer`, `duplicate-answer` (one answer per bonded key), `conclude-too-early`,
   `conclude-not-tally`, `bad-delivery-record` (a `ticket-delivery` of A6.11 malformed, sealed after `ticket-open` + `dl`/2, to an account the ticket did not draw, or not signed by the bound guardian's key; never counted).
7. **Block obedience.** A text digest is disapproved exactly when its ticket concluded `illegal`; each node adopts that into its
   own block list (Q15, Q16), never as an order that binds a node (Q51). The one exception is the guardian's authorization
   ticket of A6.11: its conclusion is the condition each node's own rule A6.11 reads to serve the profile, not an order.
8. **Miner mode on the site** reads the GPU through WebGPU with typed reasons, runs a classifier interface (a deterministic
   lexicon model today, the WebLLM adapter next) and signs the answer.
9. **Dissent and silence on the council** (spec section 9 and section 11, alternative A "Signed majority, strikes, slash
   later"). Rule: a ticket is approved by 2/3 of the consensus (at the moment only one miner answers), and an error is
   punished in the member who disagrees (later bounded by the noise rule and the numbers of A1.9). The rule is A1.8 (a dissent: paid nothing, a strike, a view
   every node computes from the signed `ticket-conclude` records) and A1.9 (the slash only above the measured noise, or for the `minor-lexicon` burn of an A6.11 ticket, or for
   silence, with the numbers `M` = 50, `D_MAX` = 10%, `S` = 3); this step states no rule of its own. Neither the strike view
   nor the slash proof is built today.

### A6. zk age: proving an age threshold without revealing the birth date (Stage 5; decision D7)

The deep design is `docs/design/2026-11-15-zk-age.md` (cite, not duplicated
here). The architecture takes the following as its frame and the choices of `docs/design/2026-11-15-zk-age.md` as the detail:

- **What is proved:** that the holder of a member key is over a threshold (16 to enter, 18 where the law asks) at a given time,
  without revealing the date of birth, and bound to the member key so the proof cannot be lent.
- **Scheme family:** an attester-signed credential plus a zero-knowledge range proof made on the device (an anonymous-credential
  scheme with range proofs, or a SNARK circuit over a signed claim). A bare hash commitment that reveals a threshold bit is the
  weaker alternative the design compares; a government identity (gov.br) as attester is the other.
- **Who verifies:** every node, from the proof record and the attester's public key, with no central server.
- **Where the proof record lives:** a signed record on the relay, owned by the member and erasable by the member like any
  key-person record (Q5); the ledger never holds it (Q4). Only the threshold result and the attester's id are visible.
- **The dependency on an attester:** a proof is only as good as who signed the credential. At the start the attester is the
  operator of the first node; later institutions through Where (A7). Revocation of an attester's key voids its credentials.
- **What the council does with content lacking the gate:** it treats it as reportable like any content (A1) and the app does
  not show age-restricted content without a valid proof.

### A7. Where for institutions (Stage 5)

The deep design is `docs/design/2026-11-15-where-institutions.md` (cite, not
duplicated here; `docs/design/2026-11-15-where-institutions.md` holds every detail). The frame: an institution (a school, a church, a company, a public body) is a **member kind**: one identity held
by several persons with roles, its own public profile (A4) and its place or places in Where. It can do what a person cannot:
issue credentials such as the age attestation of A6, publish official notices and moderate its own place. It can do nothing
more over the ledger than any member. It is a controller of its own data flows (decisions D1, D5, D6) and is attested first by
the maintainers and then by the council.
